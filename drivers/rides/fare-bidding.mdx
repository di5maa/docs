---
title: "Fare Bidding"
description: "How ride fares actually get set on Local Pass — not a fixed quote, a real back-and-forth."
---

Rides on Local Pass don't work like a typical fixed-quote rideshare app. A passenger posts what they're willing to pay for their trip, and you decide whether to take it as-is or counter.

## When a request comes in

Each request shows the passenger's **first name only** — their last name is intentionally withheld from drivers, and this is enforced by the platform, not a display choice you can turn off.

You have **45 seconds** to respond before the request automatically expires and moves on to the next available driver. A countdown ring around the timer shows how much time is left.

<Note>
  **You can have more than one request open at once.** If multiple ride invites come in around the same time, you page between them (with left/right arrows) rather than only ever seeing one at a time — each keeps its own 45-second countdown running independently.
</Note>

## Your options on a request

- **Accept** — take the fare exactly as the passenger posted it. The button reads **ACCEPT \$X.XX** and turns green when your offer matches their asking price exactly.
- **Counter** — move the price and submit an **OFFER \$X.XX** instead. The button turns black and the label changes the moment you adjust the price away from the asking amount, so you always know which action you're about to take.
- **Pass** — decline this specific request. There's no visible penalty for passing on an invite — that's different from cancelling a ride after you've already accepted it, which does affect your stats.

## Adjusting the price

Two ways to move off the passenger's asking price:

1. **Quick chips** — +5%, +10%, +15%, +25%, or +50% above the posted fare, one tap.
2. **The +/− stepper** — fine-tune by \$1.00 at a time, in either direction.

<Warning>
  **You can counter below the passenger's asking price too, not just above it** — the stepper lets you go down as well as up. There's a floor, though: if you try to go lower than the platform's minimum for that trip, the button simply won't move and you'll see _"Lowest you can offer is \$X.XX."_ That floor accounts for regulatory minimums, so it isn't something you can negotiate around.
</Warning>

## Multiple accepted offers

If you've countered on more than one request and more than one passenger accepts your offer, you'll see an **"OFFERS ACCEPTED (N)"** panel — you pick which one to actually start. Only one can become your active trip.

## Why this matters for pricing

There's no "market rate" fare shown to you the way a fixed-quote app would show one — the only number that matters is what a specific passenger is willing to pay for that specific trip. Passing on a low offer costs you nothing directly, so there's no pressure to accept a fare that doesn't work for you.
