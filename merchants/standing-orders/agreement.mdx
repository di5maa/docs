Every standing order comes with an agreement. Your customer reads it on their page and signs it with **Sign and accept**. It has two parts:

- **Your own words**: a welcome, your conditions, anything you'd put in a supply contract.
- **The terms**, filled in from the standing order itself: the products and prices, the deliveries, what can change and when, billing, and pausing or ending. You never type these. Change the standing order and they change with it, so the agreement always says what actually happens.

You can keep the terms on their own, use a template, or bring in a contract you already have.

## What's in the terms

The terms come in five parts, each with its own heading:

- **Products and prices**: a table with **Product**, **Quantity**, **Price** and **Total** (for one delivery). A product with GST on shows "plus GST" in its total. Under the table, a line says whether prices are locked (they stay as written until you change them, with your notice period) or follow your price sheet. A price change you've set for a later date is listed under "New prices from" that date.
- **Deliveries**: the days, the start date (and end date, if there is one), where it's delivered or picked up, the time window, and the delivery charge if there is one.
- **Changes and problems**: when each delivery locks, what the customer can change on their page before then, and how to report a short or damaged delivery (within 7 days, with photos if they can).
- **Billing**: how often you invoice, when payment is due, and that they pay on the invoice's payment page by card or bank, or as agreed with you.
- **Pausing or ending**: you can pause or end the agreement, and the customer asks you when they want to. Deliveries already locked still go out and are invoiced.

Here's how the products table reads for a café getting bread three mornings a week:

| Product | Quantity | Price | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sourdough loaf | 12 loaf | $6.50 per loaf | $78 |
| Butter croissant | 24 | $2.75 each | $66 |

If a product's quantity is different on some days, the quantity reads by day (for example "Mon–Thu 12, Fri 35") and the total shows the range.

## Templates

A template is your own wording around the terms: a welcome, your conditions and your signature. Templates live in **Settings → Standing orders**, under **Agreement templates**.

- **New template** starts from the Local Pass starter. **Import a PDF** starts from your own contract (see [Import your own contract](#import-your-own-contract)).
- **Edit** opens a template. The **⋯** menu has **Make default**, **Duplicate** (it opens a copy named "(copy)") and **Delete**.
- **Local Pass starter** is always at the bottom of the list, marked **Built in**. Press **Use** to make your own template from it.
- You can keep up to 20 templates. Once you have 20, **New template**, **Import a PDF** and the starter's **Use** go away, and **Duplicate** is greyed out, until you delete one.

### The default template

One template is your **Default**. Your first template becomes the default on its own, and **Make default** switches it. Each new standing order starts with a copy of the default, so the customer's agreement is ready as soon as you create it.

That copy belongs to the standing order. Editing or deleting the template later never changes an agreement already made from it ("Agreements made from it keep their copy"). If you delete your default, new standing orders start with the terms only until you pick another default.

### The Local Pass starter

The starter is a plain agreement, titled "Supply agreement". It opens with `{store} supplies {business} on a regular schedule, on the terms below.`, then all the terms, then a short **Our terms** section:

- **Prices** are in Canadian dollars and are the prices above, until you give notice of a change.
- **Delivery:** the goods are the customer's once delivered, and they should check each delivery when it arrives.
- **Payment:** each invoice is due on the date shown on it.
- **Ending this agreement:** either of you can end it with 14 days' notice in writing. Deliveries already locked still go out and are invoiced.

Then the signatures. Change any of it to suit you.

<Tip>
The starter has no late fees. If you charge interest on late invoices, write it in your own words, as a yearly rate.
</Tip>

### The preview

A template has no real customer, so its preview uses a made-up one: Café Sora (contact Mia Lee, PO-1042), getting sourdough loaves and croissants on Mon, Wed and Fri, starting next week. Your real store name and your standing order settings are used, so the cut-off, billing and notice read as yours.

## One customer's agreement

To change the agreement for one customer, open the standing order and press **Edit document**. It's in the **Agreement** card on the right and on the **Documents** tab. The page is called **Agreement document**: "What [the customer] reads and signs."

- If the standing order has no document of its own, the customer sees only the terms. The page shows them, and lists your templates, the **Local Pass starter** and **Import a PDF or paste text**. Press **Use** on one to start. Or keep the terms only: there's nothing to do.
- **Start from a template…** replaces what's there with a template, after you confirm. Nothing is saved until you press **Save**.
- **Remove document** goes back to the terms only.
- Once a standing order has ended, its document can't change. You can still read and print it.

Changes here are for this customer only. Your template stays as it was.

## The editor

Templates and agreements use the same editor. On a computer, the **Preview** sits beside it and updates as you type. On a phone, switch between **Edit** and **Preview**.

At the top is the **Title**, for example "Supply agreement". Below it, the agreement is a list of blocks. Drag a block by its handle, or use the up and down arrows, to move it. The bin removes it. **Add block** adds one at the end:

- **Heading**: a title for a part of the document.
- **Text**: your own words, with lists and fields.
- **All agreement terms**: every part of the terms, in order. Offered only while none of the parts is in the agreement.
- **Products and prices**, **Deliveries**, **Changes and problems**, **Billing**, **Pausing or ending**: one part of the terms on its own. Each is offered while it isn't in the agreement yet.

The **Signatures** block is always last. It can't be moved or removed.

### Writing text

A text block has buttons above it: **Bold**, **Italic**, **Bulleted list** and **Numbered list**. They mark your words the simple way: `**bold**`, `_italic_`, a line starting with `- ` for a list, `1. ` for a numbered list. A blank line starts a new paragraph. Pasted text comes in plain, with no fonts or colours from elsewhere.

### Insert field

**Insert field…** puts a field where your cursor is. Each customer's agreement fills it in. A field with nothing to fill in reads "—".

**About this agreement**

| Field | What it fills in |
|---|---|
| **Customer business** `{business}` | the customer's business name |
| **Customer contact** `{contact}` | the person you deal with there |
| **Customer PO number** `{po}` | their purchase order number |
| **Your store** `{store}` | your store's name |
| **Start date** `{start}` | the first day of the standing order |
| **End date** `{end}` | the last day, or "No end date" |

**From the standing order**

| Field | What it fills in |
|---|---|
| **Delivery days** `{delivery_days}` | the days, for example "Mon, Wed, Fri" |
| **Delivery dates** `{delivery_dates}` | "from" the start date, and "until" the end date if there is one |
| **Delivered to, or picked up from** `{delivery_place}` | "delivered to" the address, or "picked up from" your store, with the time window |
| **Delivery charge** `{delivery_charge}` | the charge per delivery, or "None" |
| **Cut-off time** `{cutoff}` | when each delivery locks, for example "2:00 pm the day before" |
| **What the customer can change** `{customer_can}` | for example "skip a delivery, change its quantities or pause deliveries for a while", following what you allow in your settings |
| **How often you invoice** `{invoice_every}` | "month", "week" or "two weeks" |
| **When payment is due** `{payment_due}` | for example "15 days after it is sent" |
| **Notice before a price change** `{price_notice}` | your notice period, for example "14 days" |

**Agreement terms (own line)**

| Field | What it puts there |
|---|---|
| **All agreement terms** `{terms}` | every part of the terms, with their headings |
| **Products and prices** `{products}` | the products table |
| **Deliveries** `{deliveries}` | the delivery part |
| **Changes and problems** `{changes}` | the changes part and the problems paragraph |
| **Billing** `{billing}` | the billing part |
| **Pausing or ending** `{ending}` | the pausing or ending part |

The terms fields go on a line of their own, because they become a table, a list or a few sentences. That lets you put a part of the terms right inside your own clause: write "4. Payment" as a heading, then `{billing}` on the next line. Insert field puts them on their own line for you. If one ends up inside a sentence, the editor asks you to move it.

### Split into parts and Edit wording

The **All agreement terms** block has **Split into parts**. It turns into five blocks, one per part, so you can move each one where it fits in your agreement.

**Deliveries**, **Changes and problems**, **Billing** and **Pausing or ending** each have **Edit wording**. It turns the part into a heading and a text block with the same sentences, written with fields. Change the words however you like. The days, times and amounts are still fields, so they keep following the standing order: change the cut-off or the billing period and your sentence changes with it. For example, Billing becomes:

> One invoice every `{invoice_every}` for everything delivered in it, due `{payment_due}`.

The products always stay the standing order's own: **Products and prices** has no Edit wording.

### What has to be in it

Only one thing is required: **Products and prices**, as a block, inside **All agreement terms**, or as `{products}` on a line of its own. The customer has to see what they agree to buy. Without it, the editor shows **Before you can save** and Save stays off.

The rest is up to you. If the agreement doesn't state the delivery days, the cut-off or billing (as a part of the terms or as a field), a quiet line above the blocks says so, for example "Not in this agreement yet: the cut-off and billing (optional)." It never stops you saving.

**Before you can save** also lists anything else to fix, such as an empty heading, a field name that doesn't exist ("isn't a field. Use Insert field."), or an agreement over about 15 pages. Click an item to jump to the block. A heading can be up to 120 characters and a text block up to 4,000. An agreement can have up to 80 blocks.

## Products

In one customer's agreement, the products are the standing order's real products. You can change them without leaving the document:

- In a text block, **Insert field → Products and prices** puts `{products}` on its own line and opens the products popup.
- **Edit products** opens it from the **Products and prices** block, the **All agreement terms** block, and any text block with `{products}` or `{terms}` on its own line.

The popup, **Products and prices**, is "What they get on each delivery, and the price of each." Add products, take them off, set quantities and prices, then press **Save products**. Saving changes the standing order itself:

- Deliveries that haven't locked yet follow the new list.
- The agreement's table updates, and so does the preview. Your unsaved changes to the document's words are kept.
- If the customer had already accepted, they're asked to accept again. The popup says so before you save: "Saving asks [the customer] to accept again."

On a standing order that has started, products already on it keep their price in the popup. Prices there change only from a date you choose, so the customer has notice. Use **Change prices from a date** on the standing order's edit page for that.

In a template there are no real products. Inserting `{products}` just inserts the field, and the preview shows the made-up café's bread. Each customer's agreement fills in their own.

## Import your own contract

Already have a supply contract? Bring it in instead of retyping it. In a template, press **Import** (or **Import a PDF** in Settings). In one customer's agreement, choose **Import a PDF or paste text**.

**Import your agreement**: "Start from a contract you already use. The file stays on your device."

- **Choose a PDF**: up to 10 MB. The PDF is read on your computer and never uploaded. A scanned PDF has no text to read ("Scanned pages can't be read: paste the text instead."), and a PDF with a password can't be opened: remove the password, or paste the text.
- **Or paste the text**, then press **Use this text**.

What you get:

- Headings stay headings (lines in capitals, or numbered like "3. Payment"). Paragraphs are joined back together, and bullet points become lists.
- Page numbers and blank lines for writing in ("Signature: ______") are left out. The agreement has its own signatures.
- If the contract opens with a heading that names an agreement, a contract or terms, it becomes the title. For a PDF without one, the file name is the title.
- All the terms are added at the end, then the signatures, so you can save straight away.
- Very long contracts are cut at about 15 pages, and you're told.

A green note asks you to move the terms where they fit, or put `{products}`, `{billing}` and the others on their own lines inside your clauses. Read your text through and make sure nothing in it contradicts the terms, such as prices, days or payment. Nothing is saved until you press **Save**.

## Signatures

The **Signatures** block holds both sides.

- **Your side**: type who signs, in **Signed for your store by** (for example "Ana Ruiz, Owner"), and draw your signature in the box if you like ("Sign for your store (optional)"). Draw with a finger, a pen or the mouse. **Clear** starts again. In a template, your signature is saved with it and comes along into each new agreement.
- **The customer's side** reads "Not signed yet" until they accept. They type their full name, can draw a signature, and press **Sign and accept**. Their signature then shows on the agreement with their name and the date. If they didn't draw one, their typed name stands in its place. See [What your customer sees](/merchants/standing-orders/customer-page#the-agreement).

If your customer signed a paper copy instead, upload it on the standing order's **Documents** tab with **Upload signed agreement**. Tick **Counts as accepted (signed outside Local Pass)** to count it as accepted. The file is shared with the customer on their page.

## Print or save as PDF

- In the portal: **Print** on the **Agreement** card or the **Documents** tab, or **View** (**View agreement**) and then **Print**.
- On the customer's page: **Print or save**.

Both print the agreement exactly as the customer reads it, with both signatures. To keep a PDF, choose **Save as PDF** in your browser's print window. If nothing opens, your browser blocked the window: allow pop-ups for this site and try again.

## When the customer is asked to accept again

An accepted agreement stays accepted until something in it changes. Then the customer sees "Changed since you accepted" on their page, with **Sign and accept again**. In the portal, the standing order shows **Changed since they accepted**. Deliveries carry on while they read it again.

They're asked to accept again when you change:

- the products, quantities or prices (including a price change set for a later date), GST on a product, or whether prices are locked or follow your price sheet;
- the delivery days (or how many weeks apart), start or end date, cut-off, delivery or pickup, address, time window or delivery charge;
- how often you invoice, or when payment is due;
- the customer's business name;
- the agreement's own words: any block, its title, or your signature.

Whenever a save will ask them, the page tells you first: "Saving asks [the customer] to accept again."

These don't ask them to accept again:

- renaming your store;
- your standing order settings, such as what customers may change or your price-change notice;
- the customer's contact name, email, phone or PO number;
- reminders, notes and files.
