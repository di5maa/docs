---
title: "Set up a standing order"
description: "Choose the business, its usual delivery and prices, the days, the cut-off and how it's invoiced. Then start it and send your customer their link."
---

A new standing order starts as a draft. Nothing is delivered, invoiced or sent to your customer until you press **Start**, and then only once you send them their link.

## Step by step

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose the customer">
    On **Standing orders**, press **New standing order**. Under **Who is it for?**, search your business customers and pick one, or press **Add a business customer**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the usual delivery">
    Under **Products & prices**, add what they get each time, with the quantity, unit and price. Tick **GST** on any product that carries it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the days and the cut-off">
    Under **Schedule**, choose the **Delivery days** and when it **Starts**. Under **Cut-off**, choose when each delivery locks.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose delivery or pickup, and billing">
    Check the address or pickup, any **Delivery charge**, and how often you **Invoice them** and when it's due.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start it">
    Press **Start**. If something is missing, you see **Check the highlighted sections.** and the page jumps to the first one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send them their link">
    On the standing order's page, press **Send link**. Your customer gets an email with a link to their own page.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Not ready to start? Press **Save draft**. The draft shows under **Draft** on the Standing orders page, and you can finish it later with **Edit**.

The **Summary** beside the form says the whole thing in one line, for example "Mon, Wed, Fri, 4 products, about \$210 a delivery, invoiced monthly, net 15". It also shows the first delivery and when it locks, and how many things are left to add before it can start. On a phone, the one-line summary sits in the bar at the bottom of the screen, above the buttons.

## Who is it for?

The list shows your business customers. Search by business, contact or email, then click one (or use the arrow keys and Enter). A business doesn't need a price sheet to get a standing order.

If they're not saved yet, press **Add a business customer**. It opens the same form as on [Customers](/merchants/customers). Once you save, they're picked for you.

Picked the wrong one? Press **Change** next to their name.

### Customer

The form fills in from the customer you picked. Changes here are for this standing order only.

- **Business name** and **Contact**.
- **Email for invoices**: invoices go here, and so do the codes they use to sign in to their page. It's needed to start.
- **Phone**: needed only for text reminders.
- **Also send invoices to**: up to 3 more addresses, for example their accounts team. Separate them with commas. These addresses can also get a sign-in code for the customer's page.
- **PO number** (optional): their purchase order number, printed on each invoice.

## Products & prices

This is their usual delivery: what goes out each time.

### Add products

You can add any product you sell, whether it's on your shop or unlisted.

- **Search**: type in **Add a product** and press Enter to add the one highlighted. The cursor jumps to its quantity; press Enter again to come back to the search.
- **Browse all** opens a side panel with **Ordered before** and **All products**.
- **Usually orders** shows up to 8 products this customer has ordered from your order sheets before. Press one to add it at the quantity they took last time (if it was in the same unit; otherwise it starts at 1).

A product on their price sheet starts at that sheet's unit and price. Any other product starts at its bulk price and unit if it has one, otherwise its shop price.

Each product row has:

- **Quantity**: use **−** and **+** or type it. Products sold by weight take decimals.
- **Unit**: what one unit is, such as a pound, a dozen or a case.
- **Price per** unit.
- **GST**: tick it if this product carries 5% GST. It starts unticked.
- **Total** for that product on one delivery.

If they ordered it before, the row says, for example, "Last ordered: 2 case at \$45.00 on Nov 3".

At the bottom you see how many products are on each delivery and what one delivery comes to. A standing order holds **at most 40 products**. Press **×** on a row to take a product off.

### How prices work

If you have at least one [order sheet](/merchants/order-sheet), you choose:

- **Set the prices here**: "They pay what’s written below until you change it." Prices change only when you edit the standing order.
- **Follow a price sheet**: "Each delivery uses the sheet’s price on the day it’s made." Choose the **Price sheet**. Products on that sheet take its unit and price, which you can't edit here. "A product that isn’t on the sheet keeps the price you set here." The same goes for a product the sheet prices in a different unit.

If you have no order sheets, prices are set here.

### Changing prices on a running standing order

Once a standing order has started and its prices are set here, you can't type over the price of a product already on it. Instead, press **Change prices from a date**:

1. Choose **New prices from**. It starts at your usual notice period from today.
2. Type each new price. Leave a box empty (it shows **Same**) to keep that price.
3. Press **Save changes**.

If the date is sooner than your notice period, you see a warning such as "That’s 5 days away. You usually give 14 days’ notice." It's a reminder, not a block. To drop the change, press **Cancel the price change**.

Your customer gets an email, "New prices from Mon, Dec 1", listing each old and new price. "Deliveries before then keep today’s prices." Each delivery is billed at the price on its own day, so a change in the middle of a period is shown correctly on the invoice.

A product you add to a running standing order takes the price you type, as on a new one.

**A product's unit can't change on a running standing order.** To sell it in another unit, remove it and save, then add it again in the new unit.

## Delivery or pickup

Choose **Delivery** or **Pickup**.

- **Delivery address**: search the business name or address and pick it from the list, so it shows **On the map**. A typed address that isn't picked can't start: "Pick the address from the search so it’s on the map."
- **They take deliveries only between** (or **They pick up only between** for pickup): tick it and set the times, if they only receive at certain hours. It's filled in from the customer's receiving hours when you have them.
- **Note for each delivery** (optional), such as "Back door, ask for the kitchen".

## Schedule

- **Delivery days**: tap each day they get it, **Mon** to **Sun**.
- **Repeat**: **Every week**, **Every 2 weeks**, **Every 3 weeks** or **Every 4 weeks**.
- **Starts**: tomorrow, unless you choose another date. It can't change once started.
- **Ends** (optional): leave it empty to run until you end it.

Below, **First deliveries** lists the next four. A day whose cut-off has already passed is left out: it won't be made.

## Cut-off

**Each delivery locks at** a time, on **the same day**, **the day before**, or up to **14 days before**. It starts from your settings, normally 2:00 pm the day before. You see, for example, "Tue, Nov 24 locks Mon, Nov 23, 2:00 pm. Until then you and they can change it." It says "and they" only when your [settings](/merchants/standing-orders/settings) let customers skip or change deliveries.

After the cut-off the delivery is made, and only you can change it.

On a running standing order, you can't change the cut-off in a way that would lock a delivery that's still open right now. The form tells you which day.

### Reminders

**Remind the customer before each cut-off**: **Don’t remind them**, **By email**, **By text** or **By email and text**. The reminder goes a set number of hours before each cut-off (24 unless you change it in your [settings](/merchants/standing-orders/settings#cut-off-reminders)), with a link to change or skip that delivery. Text reminders need their mobile number under **Customer**.

## Billing

- **Invoice them**: **Weekly**, **Every 2 weeks** or **Monthly**. A weekly period runs Monday to Sunday, and a monthly one is the calendar month. Every two weeks counts from the week it starts.
- **Payment**: **Due in 7 days**, **Due in 15 days** or **Due in 30 days**. If the customer has their own payment terms, those are filled in.
- **When an invoice is ready**:
  - **Use my default**: whatever your settings say.
  - **Let me check each invoice first**: it waits for you to press **Send**.
  - **Send it automatically**: "You get an email a day before it goes out."

On a running standing order, a new period or payment terms wait for the open period to finish: "The open period is invoiced as it started. The new terms apply from" the day after.

## Delivery charge

**Per delivery** (optional): a charge added to each delivery's invoice line. Leave it empty for none. It goes to you in full, with no Local Pass fee and no tax.

## Agreement

**Wait until they accept the agreement before the first delivery**: tick this if nothing should go out until your customer has accepted. Until they do, their deliveries are skipped. It's off unless you tick it. See [The agreement](/merchants/standing-orders/agreement).

## Notes

**Only you see these.** Your customer never sees them.

## Start it, and what your customer receives

Press **Start**. To start, a standing order needs the business name, an email for invoices, at least one delivery day, at least one product, and, for delivery, an address picked from the search. If you chose text reminders, it also needs their mobile number. Each delivery is then made at its cut-off.

Starting doesn't email your customer by itself. On the standing order's page, press **Send link**. If you start a draft from its own page, you're asked straight away. The box reads "Email Café Sora their standing order?" and says where it goes. Press **Send**.

Your customer gets an email from your store, for example "Green Acres Farm set up a standing order with you". It lists the products and days, from the first delivery. It says what they can do on their page (skip or change a delivery before the cut-off, and see their invoices, depending on your settings). If you ticked the agreement box, it adds "Deliveries start once you accept the agreement on your page." The **Open your standing order** button opens their page. The first time they open it on a device, they get a code by email to sign in. See [Your customer's page](/merchants/standing-orders/customer-page).

To send the link yourself, press **⋯ → Copy buyer link** and paste it into a message.

## Edit a standing order

On the standing order's page, press **Edit**. The page reads **Edit standing order with** the customer's name. Change what you need, then press **Save changes**. If you change the terms of a running standing order (products, prices, days, cut-off, delivery or billing), a customer who had accepted sees it marked as changed and is asked to accept again.

## Copy to another customer

Supplying another business the same way? Open a standing order, press **⋯** and choose **Copy to another customer**. Pick the customer, and a new draft opens: "A copy of the standing order with Café Sora. Check it before you save."

The copy brings the products and prices, how prices work, the delivery days and how often they repeat, the cut-off, delivery or pickup, the delivery charge, billing, the reminder and the agreement setting. It uses the new customer's own receiving hours and payment terms when they have them. It never copies the dates, the address, your notes, or any skipped, changed or paused days.
